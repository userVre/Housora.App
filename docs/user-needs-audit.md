# Housora User Needs and Product Coverage Audit

Reviewed: 26 August 2026

## Scope and evidence standard

This audit combines professional-practice guidance, homeowner research, competitor workflows, and product reasoning. “Complete” means the product accounts for the full residential design lifecycle; it does not mean every need belongs in the first release. Building codes, accessibility requirements, taxes, contracts, and construction documents remain jurisdiction-specific and require qualified professional review.

Primary evidence includes the [Council for Interior Design Qualification](https://www.cidq.org/about-cidq/what-is-interior-design/), [AIA design-service phases](https://www.aia.org/resource-center/defining-the-architects-basic-services), [NKBA design-process guidance](https://elearning.nkba.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/BMF15_Standard_Form_Agreement_Design_Consultation_Services.pdf), [Houzz homeowner research](https://blog.houzz.com/what-homeowners-want-during-home-renovation-projects/), [2026 Houzz renovation findings](https://www.houzz.com/press/1015/Home-Renovation-Activity-Held-Steady-in-2025-Slight-Decline-in-Plans-for-2026-Houzz-Study-Finds), [EPA remodeling and indoor-air guidance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/best-practices-indoor-air-quality-when-remodeling-your-home), and [ADA design standards](https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/design-standards/).

## Complete needs map

### 1. Discover and qualify the project

- Client, household, property, contacts, decision makers, and stakeholders
- Project goals, pain points, scope, rooms, desired outcome, target dates, and urgency
- Budget range, funding certainty, allowances, contingency, and tradeoffs
- Services requested, responsibilities, assumptions, exclusions, and deliverables
- Existing documents, photos, plans, surveys, inspiration, and product preferences
- Designer fit, trust, portfolio, credentials, communication expectations, and response times
- Proposal, agreement, deposit/retainer status, and project authorization

### 2. Understand people and daily life

- Household size, ages, routines, work-from-home, entertaining, cooking, sleep, bathing, hobbies, and storage
- Children, pets, allergies, asthma, sensory needs, mobility, vision, hearing, neurodiversity, and aging-in-place goals
- Cultural practices, privacy, religious needs, hosting patterns, and aesthetic identity
- Must-keep possessions, sentimental items, art, collections, and existing furniture
- Cleaning effort, durability, maintenance tolerance, replacement cycles, and future flexibility
- Conflicting household preferences and a clear decision/approval process

### 3. Capture the existing space accurately

- Multi-angle photos and video with quality guidance
- Dimensions, ceiling heights, wall lengths, openings, doors, windows, levels, and fixed elements
- Floor plans, elevations, reflected ceiling information, and annotations
- Orientation, daylight, views, climate, ventilation, acoustics, utilities, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and structural constraints
- Existing finishes, furniture, fixtures, equipment, damage, moisture, hazardous-material warnings, and condition notes
- Site-visit checklist, timestamp, source, confidence, unresolved questions, and verification status

### 4. Define the design program and constraints

- Functional zones, circulation, clearances, ergonomics, anthropometrics, storage, capacity, and adjacencies
- Style, palette, materials, patterns, lighting mood, visual hierarchy, and desired change intensity
- Accessibility, safety, fire/life-safety, local codes, landlord/HOA restrictions, and permit considerations
- Indoor air quality, low-emission materials, moisture resistance, ventilation, energy, water, waste, repairability, and sustainability
- Budget caps by room/category, lead-time limits, availability region, preferred vendors, and prohibited products
- Protected architecture and objects, acceptable demolition, and no-change zones

### 5. Explore and validate concepts

- Moodboards, references, palettes, style kits, and precedent images
- Multiple genuinely different concepts, not superficial style filters
- Photo-, sketch-, and floor-plan-informed generation
- Space-planning alternatives and measured feasibility checks
- Before/after, side-by-side, overlay, version history, and decision rationale
- Geometry preservation, protected-object survival, consistency across views and rooms, and confidence warnings
- Day/night, natural/artificial lighting, seasonal, and material variants

### 6. Refine the design precisely

- Semantic selection of architecture, surfaces, furniture, lighting, decor, and landscape elements
- Add, remove, replace, move, resize, rotate, recolor, rematerial, reframe, and extend
- Brush/mask edits, conversational edits, reference-driven edits, and product insertion
- Locking, constraints, undo/redo, branching versions, compare, restore, and audit trail
- Scale/dimension validation, collision/clearance warnings, and “requires professional verification” markers
- Comments and annotations attached to an image region, object, room, plan, or document

### 7. Source products and materials

- Product/material library, web clipper, uploads, favorites, tags, collections, and reusable studio standards
- Manufacturer, vendor, SKU, price, designer cost, markup, dimensions, finish, color, material, sustainability/health data, warranty, care, link, and attachment
- Availability, region, lead time, minimum order, sample status, alternates, and discontinued-item risk
- Product likeness in visualization and a clear distinction between exact product and visual approximation
- Samples, finish boards, cut sheets, schedules, and substitution workflow

### 8. Decide and approve

- Branded client presentations organized by project, room, concept, and decision
- Client-controlled simplicity; designer-controlled visibility of prices, costs, markup, notes, and alternatives
- Comments, mentions, shortlists, voting, compare, approve, decline, reason, due date, reminder, and decision history
- Multiple household decision makers and a final approver
- Scope/design/budget sign-offs and locked approved baselines
- Change requests that show impact on cost, schedule, and prior approvals

### 9. Specify, budget, and package

- Quantities, units, waste factors, room/category totals, tax, shipping, installation, contingency, allowances, and alternates
- Retail/client price; later: trade cost, markup, margin, deposits, and committed/actual cost
- Product, finish, furniture, fixture, equipment, lighting, paint, hardware, and material schedules
- Presentation, specification, cut sheet, shopping list, budget, floor-plan reference, and approval log exports
- Revision number, status, issue date, purpose, author, recipient, and traceable source links
- Branded PDF and structured spreadsheet data

### 10. Coordinate delivery and implementation

- Project phases, milestones, tasks, responsibilities, due dates, dependencies, files, meeting notes, and activity timeline
- Designer, client, teammate, architect, engineer, contractor, installer, vendor, and supplier roles with limited access
- Questions/RFIs, site issues, substitutions, change orders, punch list, installation photos, and completion status
- Bid/quote comparison, schedule impact, delivery tracking, damage/returns, and procurement later
- Final handoff, warranty/care information, as-built records, and post-occupancy feedback

### 11. Operate a professional studio

- Team roles, permissions, templates, brand kit, document numbering, studio defaults, reusable libraries, and quality checks
- Notifications, inbox, search, filters, archives, backups, activity log, export, deletion, and data portability
- Client privacy, consent, retention, model-provider disclosure, training-use disclosure, and commercial usage rights
- Usage/credit transparency, storage, billing, seat management, and support
- Integrations later: accounting, calendars, email, cloud storage, retailer catalogs, procurement, and CAD/BIM

### 12. Homeowner-specific needs

- Plain-language guided setup and education about the process
- Trustworthy expectations about what AI can and cannot verify
- Easy measurement/photo capture, automatic suggestions, and correction
- Household preference collection, shared comparison, compromise, and final decision
- Budget guardrails, realistic product alternatives, availability, lead time, maintenance, and shopping list
- Safety, accessibility, children, pets, air quality, durability, and energy prompts
- Contractor/designer handoff with all decisions and files intact
- Progress clarity and fewer technical controls; no exposure to designer-only cost/markup fields

## Product decisions

### Build into the core experience

- Client and household intake
- Space survey and verification
- Project/room hub with phases, decisions, tasks, and files
- Health, accessibility, sustainability, durability, and maintenance constraints
- Detailed specifications, lead time, alternates, and budget
- Role-based collaboration and contractor-ready handoff
- Guided homeowner mode
- Studio settings, privacy, permissions, and brand controls

### Add after visual workflow validation

- Basic tasks, milestones, RFIs, change requests, punch list, and delivery status
- Cost/markup and quote comparison
- Email/calendar/cloud-storage connections
- Sample and vendor workflows
- Post-occupancy feedback

### Integrate later rather than rebuild

- Full CAD/BIM and permit documentation
- Accounting, payments, payroll, and tax filing
- Advanced procurement, warehousing, logistics, and returns
- Contractor scheduling and field-management suite
- Code-compliance engine presented as authoritative

## Safety and trust rules

- AI output is a concept or assistance layer unless measurements and technical data are verified.
- Housora must never claim code, structural, electrical, plumbing, accessibility, fire-safety, or installation compliance without qualified review.
- Exact products and visual approximations must be clearly labeled.
- Every approved decision and exported document retains its source, revision, date, author, and status.
- Uploaded homes and client information are private by default with clear retention and deletion controls.
